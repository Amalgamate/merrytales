# Merry Tales Marketplace Governance and Trust Framework

Version 1.0 (Kenya launch baseline) — 14 August 2026

> Operational design, not legal or tax advice. Kenyan counsel, a tax adviser, the payment provider and the Data Protection Officer must approve the launch version and every material revision.

## 1. Operating position

Merry Tales should launch as a disclosed marketplace intermediary. The vendor remains the supplier of the product, rental or service and issues the customer’s compliant invoice. Merry Tales supplies marketplace tools to the vendor and issues its own eTIMS invoice for subscriptions and commissions. A payout/settlement statement does not replace either invoice.

Do not describe customer money as “escrow”, store value, or operate an internal wallet unless Kenyan payment counsel and the Central Bank of Kenya confirm the required authorization. Use a CBK-authorized payment service provider for collection, split settlement, reversals and safeguarded funds. Merchant-of-record status materially changes consumer, accounting and tax exposure and requires a separate approval.

Verification is independent of subscription. Payment buys tools and capacity; it never buys a verification badge, ranking advantage in moderation, or permission to sell a restricted item.

## 2. Stakeholders and binding documents

| Stakeholder | Must accept / receive | Core duty |
|---|---|---|
| Vendor and beneficial owners | Vendor Agreement, Seller Code, Prohibited Items Policy, Fees Schedule, Data Processing Terms | Accurate identity, licences, tax invoices, fulfilment, safety and refunds |
| Customer / event organizer | Customer Terms, Privacy Notice, Refund and Cancellation Policy | Truthful details, lawful use, payment and respectful conduct |
| Platform staff and moderators | Staff Code, Conflict Policy, Moderation Playbook, Access Policy | Consistent decisions, confidentiality, audit trail and no bribes/favouritism |
| Courier, driver, installer or subcontractor | Partner Agreement, safety and data clauses | Licensed operation, custody evidence, privacy and incident reporting |
| PSP | Processing agreement and incident/refund SLA | Authorized processing, reconciliation, fraud controls and safeguarding |
| Corporate buyer | Business Terms, procurement and authorized-user rules | Purchase authority, tax details, acceptance and timely dispute notice |
| Advertiser / affiliate | Advertising Policy | Claims must be substantiated and sponsored placement clearly labelled |

Acceptance must store user, document version, timestamp, IP/device evidence, locale, and a durable copy. Material changes require notice and, where necessary, renewed acceptance.

## 3. Vendor verification prerequisites

### Required before the first listing can be submitted

1. Identity of sole proprietor or authorized directors: Kenyan ID or passport, selfie/liveness where proportionate, phone and email verification.
2. Business evidence: BRS registration/incorporation record, legal name, registration number, physical/service address and beneficial-owner information where applicable.
3. KRA PIN matched to the vendor’s legal identity.
4. Valid KRA Tax Compliance Certificate (TCC), recorded with expiry date and independently checked. A TCC is point-in-time evidence, not a platform guarantee that every future tax is paid.
5. eTIMS onboarding evidence and declared VAT status. Where KRA’s Buyer Initiated Invoicing rules apply, the workflow must be approved by the tax adviser; it is not a blanket exemption from vendor invoicing.
6. Bank account or M-Pesa payout name match. Mismatches require evidence and enhanced review.
7. County single-business permit and category-specific licences where the activity requires them.
8. Acceptance of marketplace policies and consent/notice for verification processing.

ODPC registration is required where the vendor is legally required to register as a controller or processor. Do not falsely require a certificate from every microbusiness: statutory exemptions may apply, while specified high-risk processing may remain registrable. Every vendor must still comply with data-protection duties.

### Risk-based additions

- Catering/food: public-health/food-handler and premises evidence applicable to the county.
- Alcohol: current liquor licence and age-gated delivery; launch only after legal approval.
- Transport/limousines: vehicle ownership/authority, inspection, commercial insurance, licensed driver and relevant transport permissions.
- Security: regulator/company and guard credentials.
- Travel/accommodation: applicable tourism licensing.
- Medical/ambulance: facility/professional licences and malpractice/vehicle cover.
- Childcare: identity, background/safeguarding checks and insurance.
- Drones, pyrotechnics, tents/structures and high-risk equipment: regulator permits, competent operator and insurance.
- Branded merchandise: trademark authorization or lawful provenance.

### Renewal and monitoring

- Re-screen TCC and expiring licences 30 days before expiry; suspend new publication/payout risk features on expiry.
- Re-verify identity/ownership on material ownership, bank, phone, location or legal-name change.
- Annual full review for normal vendors; six-month review for high-risk categories.
- Trigger review for chargebacks, counterfeit notices, abnormal price/volume, duplicate identities, customer harm or sanctions/adverse information.
- Never publish ID numbers or uploaded evidence. Restrict staff access and record every view/decision.

## 4. Verification workflow and decisions

`DRAFT → PENDING_REVIEW → VERIFIED | REJECTED → SUSPENDED (when later risk arises)`

- Automation may check completeness, expiry, duplicates and name matching; a trained reviewer owns adverse decisions.
- A reviewer cannot approve their own vendor, a related party or a vendor from whom they received value.
- Record the evidence reviewed, decision reason, reviewer, time and policy version.
- “Verified” means the defined checks passed at the recorded time. It does not guarantee quality, safety, solvency or event success. Explain this next to the badge.
- Give actionable rejection reasons without disclosing anti-fraud detection logic.
- Vendor may appeal once with new evidence within 14 days; a different senior reviewer decides within 10 business days.

## 5. Listing publication workflow

`DRAFT → PENDING_REVIEW → APPROVED | CHANGES_REQUIRED | REJECTED → SUSPENDED/ARCHIVED`

Only `APPROVED + isActive` listings may appear in search, be opened publicly, or enter checkout. Editing an approved/submitted listing resets it to draft. Approval requires a verified vendor, current TCC/eTIMS status, active tools package, quota availability and any category licence.

### Listing minimum standard

- Exact product/service/package name and category; no keyword stuffing.
- Honest original/licensed images. AI imagery must not misrepresent the deliverable.
- Total price or a clearly labelled quote basis; mandatory charges, taxes, deposit and unit shown before checkout.
- Scope, quantity/capacity, service area, availability, lead time, fulfilment method and what is excluded.
- Cancellation, rescheduling, refund, damage/deposit and force-majeure terms consistent with Kenyan law.
- For vendor-created packages: itemized inclusions, optional add-ons, per-person/event limits, substitution rules, start/end time, staffing, transport, setup/teardown and overage price.
- Safety warnings, allergens, dimensions and age restrictions where applicable.
- No unverifiable superlatives, fake scarcity, fake reviews, hidden sponsored ranking or misleading comparisons.

High-risk terms/images/categories trigger manual review. Low-risk listings may receive assisted review, but random sampling and post-publication monitoring remain mandatory. Initial SLA: normal 1 business day, restricted 3 business days, urgent takedown immediately.

## 6. Catalogue policy

### Prohibited at launch

- Illegal, stolen, counterfeit or IP-infringing goods; fake tickets, certificates or identity documents.
- Firearms, ammunition, explosives, military weapons, narcotics and drug paraphernalia.
- Prescription medicines, unapproved health cures, controlled substances and unsafe medical services.
- Tobacco, nicotine/vapes, pornography, sexual services, trafficking/exploitative labour.
- Gambling/betting products, unlicensed raffles, credit/investment/insurance schemes, pyramid schemes or cryptocurrency sales.
- Wildlife products, protected species, hazardous waste/chemicals, recalled or demonstrably unsafe goods.
- Hate, violent extremist, discriminatory or deceptive political products/services.
- Spyware, stolen data, covert surveillance, account access or cyber-abuse services.
- Sale of people, organs, adoption/custody arrangements, academic cheating or bribery services.

### Restricted: disabled until category launch approval

Alcohol, prepared food, passenger transport/limousines, private security, childcare, tourism, accommodation, medical/ambulance services, pyrotechnics, drones, raffles, cosmetics with therapeutic claims, financial products, event tickets/resale and high-risk structures. Each needs a written licence matrix, reviewer checklist, incident plan, age/safety controls and counsel sign-off.

### Normally allowed after ordinary review

Invitations/printing, décor, flowers, photography/video, MC/DJ/live entertainment, venues, lawful catering after food controls, tailoring/fashion, jewellery with provenance, baby/event gifts, rentals, beauty without medical claims, corporate gifts, signage, tents/furniture/equipment after safety checks, planners and lawful professional services.

If uncertain, do not publish. Escalate to Trust & Safety. Category popularity or vendor payment never overrides this rule.

## 7. Moderation, notice and enforcement

1. Intake from automated flags, customers, rights owners, regulators, staff and vendors.
2. Preserve listing version, evidence and transaction impact.
3. Triage: imminent harm/illegal item (immediate disable); counterfeit/safety (same day); ordinary content (two business days).
4. Decide using a reason code and written explanation: approve, request changes, reject, suspend or terminate.
5. Notify affected parties and refund/hold/reconcile impacted orders lawfully.
6. Appeals decided by a different reviewer. Emergency suspensions remain during appeal where harm risk is credible.

Strike guide: education for minor first error; warning/temporary listing restriction for repeated negligence; immediate suspension for fraud, counterfeit, serious safety harm, bribery, evasion or illegal goods. Linked-account evasion is grounds for termination. Preserve regulator/litigation evidence under a documented legal hold.

## 8. Customer protection and anti-lock-in

Trust should create retention, not captivity:

- No vendor exclusivity by default; vendors may multi-home and export their catalogue/order/customer data subject to privacy law.
- Customers can download receipts, quotes, contracts and event plans; close accounts and request lawful deletion.
- Monthly subscription can be cancelled without hidden steps; access continues through the paid period. No silent tier upgrades.
- Search ranking criteria and sponsored results are labelled. Subscription tier may unlock tools, but cannot purchase verification or conceal lower relevance.
- Publish seller legal/trading identity, location/service area, contact channel, full price, delivery timing and cancellation/refund rules before purchase.
- Provide order acknowledgement and durable terms. Resolve non-delivery, materially misdescribed supply, unsafe goods and duplicate payment quickly.
- Do not prohibit lawful complaints, chargebacks, regulator reports or honest reviews. Detect review manipulation and retaliation.
- Permit off-platform communication needed to perform an order, but warn against off-platform payment fraud. Do not misuse customer data for unrelated marketing.

## 9. Refunds, cancellation and disputes

- Separate platform-tool subscriptions from event purchases.
- Subscription refund rule: correct duplicate/failed provisioning immediately; cancellation prevents renewal; legally required remedies override policy.
- Vendor package terms must distinguish refundable booking deposit, security/damage deposit and non-refundable work already performed. Avoid calling every advance “non-refundable”.
- Use milestone acceptance for custom print/design and event services. Preserve proofs and approvals.
- Dispute ladder: self-resolution (48 hours), platform mediation (5 business days), specialist escalation, then court/ADR rights. Platform policy cannot remove statutory rights.
- Vendor payout reserves must be risk-based, disclosed, time-limited and reconciled; never arbitrary.

## 10. Packages, pricing and unit economics

Recommended monthly tools packages (Kenyan shillings, tax treatment shown separately at checkout):

| Tier | Fee | Listings | Seats | Suggested marketplace commission | Main tools |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Starter | 100 | 5 | 1 | 5% | Shop, quotes, basic analytics |
| Growth | 300 | 25 | 2 | 4% | Packages, availability, promotions |
| Business | 600 | 100 | 5 | 3% | Bulk tools, team access, advanced analytics |
| Pro | 1,000 | 500 fair-use | 10 | 2.5% | Corporate RFQs, branches, exports/integrations |

These fees are intentionally accessible but cannot fund a high-touch trust operation alone. At 1,000 vendors with a KES 350 blended monthly fee, subscription MRR is only KES 350,000 before tax, payment fees, support, verification and infrastructure. Sustainable revenue therefore needs transparent transaction commission and optional value-added services (promoted placement, SMS units, delivery, financing referral only where authorized), never hidden checkout fees.

Illustrative marketplace value: at KES 10 million monthly GMV and a blended 4% commission, commission revenue is KES 400,000; combined with the illustrative KES 350,000 subscriptions, gross platform revenue is KES 750,000 before PSP costs, refunds, taxes and operating expenses. Treat this as scenario math, not a forecast. Measure GMV, take rate, contribution margin/order, CAC, repeat rate, dispute/refund rate, fraud loss, verification cost and vendor time-to-first-sale.

Vendor-created packages are customer offers, not platform subscription tiers. Enforce package schemas and moderation on each package; never let vendors label an offer “insured”, “licensed”, “guaranteed” or “official” without evidence.

## 11. Tax controls

- Collect and validate vendor KRA PIN/TCC/eTIMS evidence; store expiry and reviewer audit, not public document copies.
- Vendor issues the supply invoice to the customer; Merry Tales issues vendor invoices for subscription, commission and add-ons through eTIMS.
- Settlement reports show gross sale, refund, PSP fee, platform commission, applicable tax and net payout.
- Maintain vendor legal name/PIN on immutable transaction snapshots even if the profile later changes.
- Reconcile PSP, bank, orders, invoices, credit notes, refunds and payouts daily; investigate differences.
- Determine VAT, withholding obligations and platform-specific tax treatment with a Kenyan tax professional before launch and after Finance Act changes.
- If integrating directly with eTIMS, complete KRA’s system-to-system certification. Do not invent invoice numbers or claim KRA verification from a document upload alone.
- Report suspected forged TCC/PIN/invoices, preserve evidence and suspend publication pending review.

## 12. Privacy and security

Apply purpose limitation, minimization, retention schedules, encryption, MFA for staff, least privilege, access logs, vendor/customer separation, processor contracts, breach response and cross-border safeguards. Verification documents should use private object storage, malware scanning, signed short-lived access and automatic retention review. Never store raw card credentials. Establish deletion schedules subject to tax, dispute, fraud and legal-hold needs.

High-risk profiling, biometrics/liveness or automated adverse decisions require a data-protection impact assessment and human review path. Marketing consent must be separate from marketplace terms.

## 13. Governance ownership and launch gates

- Board/CEO: risk appetite and restricted-category launches.
- Compliance/Legal: laws, regulator engagement and policy versions.
- Tax lead/adviser: invoice model, eTIMS, VAT/withholding and reconciliation.
- Trust & Safety: verification/moderation, incident response and transparency reporting.
- DPO: privacy programme, DPIAs, rights and breach handling.
- Finance: PSP settlement, reserves, refunds and revenue recognition.
- Product/Engineering: enforce server-side gates, audit logs, expiry jobs and access controls.

Do not go live until: contracts are counsel-approved; tax flow is signed off; PSP arrangement is approved; ODPC position documented; all public APIs enforce approval; checkout rejects unapproved listings; staff RBAC/MFA and four-eyes review exist; refund/incident/takedown drills pass; backups/restore and breach exercises pass; restricted categories are disabled by default.

## 14. Required operational artefacts

Before production create: Vendor Agreement; Customer Terms; Privacy/Cookie Notices; Fees Schedule; Prohibited/Restricted Items Policy; Refund/Cancellation Policy; IP notice-and-takedown form; moderation reason-code catalogue; category licence matrix; verification checklist; reviewer conflict declaration; appeals form; law-enforcement request procedure; data retention schedule; incident/breach playbook; payout/reconciliation SOP; tax/eTIMS SOP; quarterly transparency report template.

## 15. Source baseline for counsel review

- KRA eTIMS and Buyer Initiated Invoicing guidance
- Kenya Consumer Protection Act, 2012
- Kenya Data Protection Act, 2019 and ODPC registration/compliance guidance
- Anti-Counterfeit Act
- National Payment System Act and CBK payment-provider requirements
- Applicable county, sector, IP, public-health, transport, tourism, security and safety rules

Review this framework at least quarterly and whenever Kenyan law, KRA guidance, payment arrangements or catalogue scope changes.
